# Mathematics Grade-Level Progressions

## What This Reference Solves

Teachers and instructional designers often need to place mathematics material at the right **depth** for a learner’s current grade or readiness band—not only whether a topic appears on a standards list, but whether to **introduce**, **consolidate**, **deepen**, or **transfer** an idea. This handbook supports that decision for school mathematics from early elementary through high school, with emphasis on learners who already have basic number knowledge.

It does **not** provide a complete K–12 topic sequence, grade-by-grade standards map, or lesson plans. It explains how mathematical ideas typically **build across grades** and how to judge **surface**, **deep**, and **transfer** work using evidence from student performance.

For preschool and primary **whole-number sense** (counting levels, Number Knowledge bands, ages roughly 4–8), use `early-number-knowledge.md`. For warnings about treating disconnected scope-and-sequence charts as placement truth, see `scope-sequence-limits.md`.

## Grade Bands and Domains Covered

| Band | Typical grades | Focus in this handbook |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| Early elementary | K–2 | Notation, operations, measurement foundations; bridge from number sense |
| Upper elementary | 3–5 | Multiplicative reasoning, fractions, area/volume, early generalization |
| Middle school | 6–8 | Rational number, proportional reasoning, integers, functions as patterns |
| High school | 9–12 | Formal algebra, functions in context, modeling across disciplines |

**Confidence:** Grade labels describe common school groupings, not fixed developmental ceilings. Individual learners often sit above or below band norms on specific ideas.

**Out of scope for illustrated progressions:** Geometry (including proofs), statistics, and probability do not have band-specific material tables in this handbook. Use the generic placement decision flow and phase diagnostics below, plus `grade-band-material-leveling.md` for band scaling and `developmental-readiness-prerequisites.md` for readiness—not timing inferred from the multiplication, rational-number, and algebra clusters alone.

## Grounding Tradition

Three ideas from the source corpus anchor placement:

1. **Learning is recursive, not linear.** Surface, deep, and transfer phases **spiral** within a lesson, unit, and year. The right instructional move depends on where the learner is on a topic, not on the calendar alone.
2. **Mathematics progresses as connected networks.** Ideas at one grade (e.g., equal groups, arrays, area) should later connect to new topics (e.g., division, fraction operations, volume formulas). Placement should preserve those links, not isolated objectives.
3. **Depth is distinct from difficulty.** More problems or harder numbers do not equal deeper learning; richer structure, comparison, and generalization signal depth. Transfer requires noticing similarities and differences across situations.

## Surface, Deep, and Transfer: Definitions for Placement

### Surface (initial acquisition and consolidation)

**Material:** New concepts introduced with meaning; vocabulary and notation that name ideas students have already explored; procedures practiced with conceptual grounding; representations (manipulatives, diagrams, number lines) linked to symbols.

**Depth target:** Recognition, accurate use in familiar settings, efficient retrieval of facts and conventions so working memory is free for harder thinking.

**Not surface:** Rote algorithms with no understanding, or treating “surface” as “shallow.”

**Typical signals the learner is still in surface phase**

- Needs models or worked examples for every instance of a type of problem.
- Can perform a procedure in isolation but cannot explain what quantities mean.
- Errors show missing vocabulary, notation, or fact retrieval—not yet wrong structure.

**Placement move:** Introduce or consolidate. Use smaller numbers, clearer contexts, spaced practice over days, number talks, guided questions—not premature “application packs.”

### Deep (relationships and generalization)

**Material:** Tasks that require comparing representations, justifying claims, finding structure (patterns, invariants), relating ideas (multiplication and division, area and arrays, slope and rate), and mathematical discourse (explain, critique, restate).

**Depth target:** Flexible understanding—knowing **which** tool fits, not only **how** to execute one procedure.

**Typical signals the learner is ready to deepen**

- Surface skills are **sufficient**: can represent the idea multiple ways and retrieve basics without exhausting attention.
- Can notice repetition (e.g., place-value shifts when multiplying by multiples of ten) and articulate **why** a pattern holds.
- Engages in explanation beyond step recounting; compares strategies.

**Placement move:** Shift from exercise sets to rich problems, open middles, accountable talk, comparison of methods. Pull back to brief surface support if struggle is about missing facts or notation, not structure.

### Transfer (application and self-directed use)

**Material:** Novel contexts (other subjects, real settings), multi-step modeling, problems where operation or model is not given, metacognitive prompts (compare to prior problem, choose representation), peer explanation that forces reorganization of knowledge.

**Depth target:** Appropriate application in unfamiliar situations; detecting when a new problem is like or unlike prior ones; metacognitive monitoring.

**Near vs. far transfer (placement heuristic)**

| Type | Leap to new situation | Example progression (paraphrased) |
|------|------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Near | Close context or representation change | Equal-group models → array models → area as arrays; same operation family, new diagram |
| Far | Distant context or number system | Whole-number division meaning (“how many groups?”) → fraction division in a measurement context years later |

**Typical signals the learner is ready to transfer**

- Deep understanding on **near** variants: chooses correct operation or model in varied wordings of the same structure.
- Can pause to compare a new problem to a recent one before calculating.
- Explains decisions with evidence, not only answers.

**Typical signs transfer is premature**

- Strong on exercises but freezes on applications with mixed operations.
- Applies a memorized rule without checking whether the situation matches (e.g., fraction procedures with no sense of quantity).
- “Transfer” that is really misapplied surface knowledge—wrong analogy, no similarity/difference check.

**Placement move:** Context-rich tasks, cross-disciplinary use, student-selected strategies, checklists for self-questioning. If failure is structural, return to deep or surface—not more drill of the wrong type.

### Cyclical rule for daily decisions

Within one topic, expect movement **surface → deep → transfer → surface** (new sub-idea) repeatedly. Do not assume “unit week 1 = surface only.” Judge by evidence on **this** idea for **this** learner.

**Learning definition (placement summary):** Sufficient surface knowledge → deeper understanding → appropriate transfer to new tasks. All three are legitimate goals; stalling at surface only is a common misplacement.

## How Ideas Build Across Grades (Illustrative, Not Exhaustive)

The patterns below show **conceptual stacking** and **depth expectations**. They are **not** a full curriculum. Local standards still list topics; this section explains **readiness and depth**, not “age X must cover topic Y” with precision.

### Multiplicative reasoning (roughly grades 2–5)

| Stage | Typical grade band | Prior foundation | Material emphasis | Depth phase |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Equal groups and repeated addition | 2–3 | Addition, skip counting | Physical equal groups; linking addition strings to multiplication notation | Surface → early deep |
| Arrays, commutativity, area link | 3–4 | Equal-group fluency | Arrays in context (grids, area); order of factors vs. appearance of arrays | Deep |
| Facts and efficiency | 3–5 | Models understood | Spaced practice, number relationships—not speed races divorced from meaning | Surface consolidation |
| Division as inverse / grouping | 3–5 | Multiplication models | Connecting multiplication and division situations | Deep → near transfer |
| Area and perimeter | 4–5 | Arrays, factors | Rectangles; linking to prior array work | Deep → transfer |

**Misplacement:** Memorizing fact tables before equal-group meaning; treating area formulas as new magic unrelated to multiplication.

**Diagnostic (placement):** Given a grouping situation with small numbers, can the learner draw or describe groups **and** write a matching equation before fact drills?

### Rational number and operations (middle grades)

| Stage | Typical grade band | Prior foundation | Material emphasis | Depth phase |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Fraction as quantity | 3–4 | Whole-number part-whole | Number line, area models | Surface → deep |
| Fraction operations with meaning | 5–7 | Whole-number division sense | Why procedures match situations; magnitude sense | Deep |
| Decimals, percent, ratio | 6–8 | Fraction understanding | Connecting representations; avoiding disconnected shortcuts | Deep → transfer |
| Integers | 6–7 | Whole-number operations | Signed quantities in context; linking to known operations | Surface → deep |

**Cross-grade caution:** Learners who learned “invert and multiply” without division-as-grouping often cannot transfer to fraction word problems. Placement should verify **whole-number division meaning** (often solid by upper elementary) before treating fraction division as procedural only.

### Variables, functions, and modeling (late middle → high school)

| Stage | Typical grade band | Prior foundation | Material emphasis | Depth phase |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Notation and conventions | 6–8 | Arithmetic fluency | Symbols record known reasoning; slope-intercept as labels tied to graphs | Surface |
| Structure and rate | 7–9 | Ratio, linear patterns | Slope as rate of change across tables, graphs, contexts | Deep |
| Functions across disciplines | 9–12 | Linear/exponential core | Science/economics contexts; choosing model | Transfer |

**Misplacement:** Algebra as symbol manipulation without prior ratio and graph sense; modeling tasks before notation is consolidated.

## Placement Decision Flow

Use this when choosing material for a topic at a stated grade or readiness level:

1. **Name the idea** (e.g., “volume of rectangular prisms,” “operating with negative numbers”).
2. **List prerequisites** as networks, not single skills (e.g., multiplication fluency **and** 3D measurement language **and** composing/decomposing shapes).
3. **Diagnose phase** with a short task in a **new-but-related** context (see diagnostics below).
4. **Match task type to phase:**
   - Surface: low complexity, clear models, introduce notation after meaning.
   - Deep: multiple representations, justify, generalize.
   - Transfer: context chosen for relevance; operation not signaled; compare/contrast prompt.
5. **Plan the next step** if successful (near transfer before far; next grade’s connection).

**Task-type quick map**

| Phase | Task types to favor | Task types to avoid as primary |
|-------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| Surface | Manipulatives, number talks, worked examples with “why,” guided discovery | Timed speed drills; long context-free drill |
| Deep | Open middles, compare representations, structure prompts | Only one-step exercises |
| Transfer | Modeling, cross-curricular problems, student strategy choice | Clones of yesterday’s worksheet with new numbers only |

**Difficulty vs. complexity:** Raising page count or number size is **difficulty**; raising connections, steps of reasoning, or abstraction is **complexity**. Deep and transfer placement should increase **complexity** first; reserve higher **difficulty** for consolidation after meaning is secure.

## Readiness Diagnostics (Sample Patterns)

Design checks that reveal **phase**, not only right/wrong. Paraphrased task patterns—adapt numbers and contexts to your band.

### Multiplication / division placement

- **Surface check:** Model an equal-group situation and write an equation; explain what each factor means.
- **Deep check:** Two correct solutions for the same total (e.g., coin combinations); prove all solutions found; relate array to area.
- **Transfer check:** Unfamiliar context (purchase, scheduling) with no keyword training; learner selects operation and defends choice.

### Fraction / rational placement

- **Surface check:** Locate a fraction on a number line; compare two fractions with reasoning, not only a cross-multiply trick.
- **Deep check:** Explain why a procedure works in one representation (area or number line).
- **Transfer check:** Mixed operation word problems; learner compares to a known whole-number division situation before computing.

### Volume / measurement (upper elementary)

- **Surface check:** Fill or count units to find volume of one prism.
- **Deep check:** Find all whole-number edge lengths for a fixed volume; debate whether reordering dimensions changes the “box.”
- **Transfer check:** Composite solid or real packaging problem requiring decomposition and justification.

### Proportional reasoning (middle grades)

- **Surface check:** In a familiar mixture context (e.g., a recipe), state the ratio of two ingredients with units; write an equivalent ratio using a representation the learner already trusts (table, double number line, or common-denominator fractions).
- **Deep check:** When one quantity in a mixture is wrong, scale the other parts to restore the original ratio; justify why the same multiplier on both parts preserves the relationship—not only a cross-multiply procedure.
- **Transfer check:** Unfamiliar rate or mixture problem with no operation keywords; learner compares the structure to a known whole-number division or fraction story, then defends whether the situation is proportional before computing.

### Algebra / functions (secondary)

- **Surface check:** Interpret parameters in a familiar graph context.
- **Deep check:** Parallel lines in different pricing representations—explain **why** parallelism implies same rate.
- **Transfer check:** Multi-day modeling task (e.g., finance, science data) with recommendation justified by mathematics.

**Interpretation:** Failure at deep tasks with strong surface scores → stay in deep with targeted surface repair. Failure at transfer with strong calculation → missing structure or similarity detection, not more calculation practice.

## Learners Ahead, Behind, or Uneven

**Behind on surface:** Do not jump to transfer problems “because they’re in grade X.” Use same-age social context sparingly; prioritize missing representations and vocabulary, then re-enter deep.

**Ahead on surface only:** Can compute but not explain or apply → place in **deep**, not skip ahead on curriculum lists.

**Ahead on deep:** Offer near then far transfer; peer tutoring (explaining reorganizes knowledge) can consolidate—but tutor needs structure and training.

**Uneven across topics:** Normal. Progressions are **topic-specific**; a seventh grader may be in transfer for integers but surface for proportional reasoning. Diagnose per idea, not per label.

**Grade vs. age:** Primary sources stress typical understandings for **class age groups** while acknowledging wide spread. Placement uses **knowledge phase on the idea**, with grade as a default hypothesis only.

## Common Misplacement

| Error | What goes wrong | Better placement |
|-------|-----------------|------------------|
| Surface-only year | Facts and worksheets without connection | Spiral deep tasks weekly on same topic |
| Deep without surface | Rich tasks while notation/facts absent | Brief surface repair, then return to deep |
| Fake transfer | Keywords in word problems; one template per lesson | Compare problems; withhold operation cues |
| Curriculum racing | Next grade’s topic before network in place | Teach prerequisites explicitly (e.g., division meaning before fraction division) |
| Confusing rigor with volume | More exercises, harder numbers | More structure, justification, representation |
| Timed fluency as gate | Anxiety; speed ≠ fluency | Flexible, accurate, efficient thinking in varied contexts |
| Mnemonics without meaning | PEMDAS-style tricks without structure | Mnemonic only after conceptual anchor |
| Isolated objectives | “This week: chapter 7” with no link to prior arrays/factors | Name the connection when placing material |

## Worked Placement Reasoning (Paraphrased Scenarios)

**Scenario A — Third grade, introducing multiplication.**  
Class has skip counting and simple arrays from prior years. **Placement:** Surface—equal-group contexts, delayed formal learning intention until after modeling, then notation and vocabulary. **Not yet:** Multi-step mixed applications requiring operation choice. **Next:** Arrays and fact strategies (surface/deep spiral), then area as transfer context later in the year.

**Scenario B — Fifth grade, volume unit.**  
Prerequisite: multiplication and measurement language. **Placement:** Start with deep exploration (packing cubes, dimension tables), then connect pattern to formula (surface notation for deep idea), then transfer to composite figures or real containers. **Misplacement:** Lead with formula worksheet before packing experience.

**Scenario C — Student aces fraction computation, fails applications.**  
**Diagnosis:** Surface/deep without transfer (classic “Linda” pattern: knows how and why of procedure, cannot map situations). **Placement:** Near transfer with compare/contrast to whole-number division stories; pause before new fraction rules. **Not:** More identical computation pages.

**Scenario D — High school exponential functions.**  
**Placement:** Transfer task (multi-day modeling, judgment, evidence) only if surface (notation, parameters) and deep (rate, compounding meaning) are in place. **Prerequisite check:** Linear function and ratio understanding.

## Confidence and Limits

| Claim | Confidence | Limit |
|-------|------------|-------|
| Surface / deep / transfer as placement phases | High | Phases overlap; not a rigid lesson script |
| Recursive, spiraling learning | High | Does not specify daily lesson order |
| Multiplication → division → fraction transfer chain | Medium–high | Timing varies by curriculum and learner |
| Grade bands as typical grouping | Medium | Wide individual variation; not developmental ceilings |
| Exact topic per grade nationwide | Low | Sources give depth guidance and examples, not authoritative scope-and-sequence |
| Age X must learn topic Y | Low | Use diagnostics, not age alone |

**What this corpus does not justify**

- A complete ordered list of all K–12 mathematics topics.
- Replacing teacher judgment with a single placement test score.
- Claims that children “cannot” learn an idea before a grade if evidence shows conceptual readiness.
- Copying commercial progression charts without checking conceptual networks (see `scope-sequence-limits.md`).
