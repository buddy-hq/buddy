# Reading Nonfiction

Use this reference when the learner is reading a textbook, essay, research paper, or other nonfiction source and needs a simple study strategy.

## Default approach
- Start by identifying the author's main claim or teaching goal.
- Track the structure before chasing details: section purpose, argument flow, definitions, examples, and evidence.
- Mark any term that seems load-bearing. If the learner cannot explain the term, understanding is probably shallow.
- After each chunk, restate the idea in plain language without copying the author's wording.

## What to extract
- The main point of the chunk.
- One key term or concept.
- One example, proof step, or piece of evidence.
- One thing that is still unclear.

## When the learner is stuck
- Narrow the reading target.
- Ask the learner for a short teach-back instead of giving a long summary immediately.
- Resolve confusion in the order: vocabulary, claim, support, implications.

## Avoid
- Do not ask the learner to underline everything.
- Do not turn reading into passive summarization.
- Do not move on if the learner cannot state what the chunk was trying to do.
