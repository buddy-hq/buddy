# ChatGPT Codex for Work — page structure

> Source: https://chatgpt.com/codex/for-work · Fetched: 2026-06-24

**Product:** Codex — OpenAI's AI assistant for work. This is the work/business-focused sub-page.
**Page goal:** Get teams onto ChatGPT Business plan. "Get Codex for your team."
**Tone:** Practical, task-oriented, "get work done." Less technical than the developers page.
**Visual:** Clean, blue-tinted. Prompt card screenshots showing real task examples. Same nav/footer.

---

## Sections (top to bottom)

### Nav (same as all Codex pages)
- Massive ChatGPT nav with Codex dropdown. Contact sales · Log in.

### Hero
- **Headline:** "Work faster with Codex"
- **Subhead:** "Codex is your personal assistant for work. It researches, organizes, drafts, analyzes, and automates recurring tasks, using your tools."
- **CTA:** "Get Codex for your team"
- **Visual:** Animated/loading product visual.

### "Built for teams getting work done with OpenAI" (3 feature blocks)
- **Heading:** "Built for teams getting work done with OpenAI"
- **Block 1 — Create docs, spreadsheets, and slides in minutes:** "Tell Codex where to look, and it will do the research then create summaries, decks, emails, and reports for you in minutes."
- **Block 2 — Automate recurring tasks:** "Delegate repeat work so the next update, draft, review packet, or follow-up is ready when the team needs it."
- **Block 3 — Focus on the work that matters:** "Hand off the toil so you can focus on what really matters for your business, like talking to customers, making decisions, and setting strategy."
- **Note:** 3 short blocks. Each is a bold headline + 1-sentence desc. No screenshots. Focuses on outcomes (create, automate, focus), not features.

### "Your personal assistant" (4-step loop with prompt card visuals)
- **Heading:** "Your personal assistant"
- **Intro:** "Codex helps you get work done while you stay in charge. Give it the context, choose the output, and decide what gets used, sent, shared, or automated."
- **Step 1 — Start with real tasks:** "Pick something your team already has to do: a launch brief, customer summary, KPI readout, or follow-up. Build trust one output at a time." Visual: prompt card — "Create a leadership brief from Google Calendar, Slack, and Google Drive context."
- **Step 2 — Codex gathers the right data:** "Let Codex find the docs, spreadsheets, messages, notes, and screenshots it needs. Keep the scope clear and focused on the task." Visual: prompt card — "Read interviews and survey notes from Outlook Email and SharePoint."
- **Step 3 — Automate:** "Ask Codex to share a weekly update every Wednesday or stay on top of customer feedback." Visual: prompt card — "Draft a team update based on various inputs" with scheduling.
- **Step 4 — You are in control:** "Codex asks for permission before taking action and cites its work. You decide what ships and when the work is finished." Visual: prompt card — "Draft a launch narrative and team update from docs, Notion notes, and timelines."
- **Note:** 4-step workflow loop. Each step has a prompt card screenshot showing a real example. The "you are in control" step mirrors Dia's privacy/control pattern.

### "Ways teams use Codex" (8 example prompts, carousel)
- **Heading:** "Ways teams use Codex"
- **Subhead:** "Start with work your team already has to do."
- **8 example prompts (each is a title + 1-sentence task description):**
  1. **Weekly brief** — "Turn my calendar, messages, and docs into a weekly brief with priorities, risks, decisions, and follow-ups."
  2. **Survey feedback** — "Analyze this survey feedback in Google Sheets. Pull themes, representative quotes, open questions, and a short team update."
  3. **Email replies** — "Find the emails I need to answer. Summarize each one, draft replies, and leave them unsent for review."
  4. **Launch brief** — "Turn this product doc, launch timeline, and Slack thread into a launch brief with owners, risks, decisions, and next steps."
  5. **Attendee list** — "Build an attendee list from these leaders, draft the invites, create a tracker, and flag anything that needs manual review."
  6. **Training follow-ups** — "Scan this training spreadsheet for employees below 100% completion, then draft Slack DMs using the template in this doc."
  7. **Pipeline review** — "Compare this quarter's pipeline spreadsheet against last week's forecast. Summarize changes, risks, assumptions, and follow-ups."
  8. **Project tracker** — "Create a project tracker from this planning doc, assign likely owners from the discussion, and draft a status update for the team channel."
- **Note:** This is the strongest section — concrete, copy-paste-ready examples of what to ask Codex. Each is a real task teams do today. Repeated 3x in the fetch (likely a looping carousel). Maps to Raycast's extension-showcase pattern: examples ARE the pitch.

## Pattern summary

| Aspect | Observation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Narrative** | Positioning headline → 3 outcome blocks (create, automate, focus) → 4-step workflow loop with prompt card visuals → 8 example prompts carousel → resources → final CTA |
| **Core pattern** | Examples-as-pitch. The "Ways teams use Codex" section (8 concrete example prompts) does the selling — not feature lists. Similar to Raycast's extension showcase. The 4-step loop (start → gather → automate → control) is a mini narrative within the page. |
| **Trust signals** | None — no testimonials, no logos, no social proof. Trust comes from the concrete examples being recognizable as real work. |
| **Conversion** | Single CTA: "Get Codex for your team" (repeated in hero + final). Links to pricing. No download. |
| **Distinctive** | The 8-example prompt carousel is the strongest section — concrete, recognizable, copy-paste-ready. No testimonials at all (unlike the developers page which has 6). The 4-step "you are in control" loop mirrors Dia's privacy-as-control pattern. Lighter on features, heavier on "here's what to actually do with it." |
| **Relevance to Buddy** | The example-prompt-carousel pattern maps directly to Buddy: "Ways learners use Buddy" (import a textbook → generate flashcards; ask about a concept → get Socratic questions; read a chapter → auto-summarize notes). The 4-step control loop maps to Buddy's "you stay in control" (local-first, your data, your keys). |
