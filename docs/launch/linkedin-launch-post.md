# LinkedIn launch post

Working document for choosing and drafting Buddy's LinkedIn launch post.

## Research conclusion

The recurring structure in the launch posts reviewed was:

**announcement → problem or origin → product definition → proof → availability → action**

Buddy must be explicitly announced within the first one to four lines. The launch film provides the
visual proof. The post explains why Buddy exists and gives the reader one action.

## Recommendation

Use the founder-origin narrative. Coding agents changed how you learned, but their environment was
made for code. You built the learning-specific agent you wanted.

Combine it with one concrete question from narrative 4 and the honest feedback invitation from
narrative 5.

## Five dummy posts

These are complete dummy posts for comparing the five narratives. They are not final copy.

### 1. The agent I wanted for learning

Today, I am launching Buddy, a free desktop AI agent for the curious.

I started building Buddy after coding agents changed how I learned. They could search, use tools,
create files, and keep working through a difficult problem with me. Learning suddenly felt less
like asking a chatbot for an answer and more like working beside someone capable.

Those agents were made for code. My learning kept pulling me elsewhere: into books, notes,
whiteboards, research papers, browser tabs, and small experiments that made an idea click. I wanted
one agent that could work across all of them.

That became Buddy.

You can open a PDF or EPUB beside the conversation, bring in your existing Obsidian vault, ask Buddy
to research a question, think through it on a whiteboard, or turn an explanation into a simulation.
Quizzes and flashcards are there when you want to remember what you found.

Buddy is local-first. The notes and files it creates stay on your computer in formats you own. You
can connect ChatGPT, use an API key, or run a local model.

It is available today for macOS and Windows. No account. No subscription to Buddy.

Download it at hibuddy.in.

If you try it, bring one book, vault, or question you already care about. I would like to know what
you ask Buddy to do first.

### 2. Problem first, then launch

Today, I am launching Buddy, an AI agent that keeps the different parts of learning around the same
question.

My learning rarely stays in one app. A question starts in a book, moves into Obsidian, opens a row of
browser tabs, becomes a rough diagram, and ends up split across several AI conversations. Each tool
helps, but the thread between them is mine to carry.

I built Buddy so the thread can stay in one workspace.

Open a PDF or EPUB and ask about the passage in front of you. Open the Obsidian vault you already
use, with its wikilinks, embeds, and Markdown files intact. Send a question into research, sketch the
answer on a whiteboard, or ask Buddy to build a simulation you can change and play with.

The result does not have to disappear into chat history. Buddy can write what you learned back into
files on your computer. Your material remains yours, and you can connect the AI subscription, API,
or local model you already prefer.

Buddy is free to download today for macOS and Windows. It does not require an account.

You can watch the launch film below and get Buddy at hibuddy.in.

Start with something already sitting unfinished in your reading list or notes. That is the workflow
I built Buddy for.

### 3. The pleasure of finding things out

Today, I am launching Buddy, a free desktop AI agent for the curious.

Buddy gives that process an agent and a workspace.

Bring a PDF, an EPUB, or an existing Obsidian vault. Buddy can read beside you, research the gaps,
draw on a whiteboard, build an interactive simulation, and turn what you found into notes, quizzes,

The files remain on your computer. You choose the AI behind Buddy by connecting a subscription, an

Buddy is free and available today for macOS and Windows. No account is required.

Watch the film, then download Buddy at hibuddy.in.

Bring it the question you keep returning to. The point is to enjoy finding out what happens next.

### 4. Follow one question

Today, I am launching Buddy, an AI agent designed to follow a question wherever learning takes it.

The first question in the launch film is simple: Why does the Moon not fall down?

Answering it properly can take more than a chat response. You may want the visual explanation, the
page in a physics book, a web search for the part that still feels wrong, a diagram you can edit, or
a simulation that lets you change the variables yourself.

Buddy can move through that whole journey with you.

It can read a PDF or EPUB beside the conversation. It can work with the notes already in your
Obsidian vault. It can research a branch of the question, sketch on a whiteboard, build something
interactive, and save the useful result into files you own.

I built Buddy because the question should remain visible even when the form of learning changes.
The book, research, drawing, experiment, and notes should feel like parts of the same thought.

Buddy is local-first and lets you use ChatGPT, an API key, or a local model. The app itself is free,
requires no account, and is available today for macOS and Windows.

Download it at hibuddy.in and give it one question you have wanted to understand for a long time.

### 5. The public experiment

Today, I am launching Buddy, a free desktop AI agent for the curious, and opening it up to the people
who will help me discover what it should become.

I started with a broad belief: learning deserves the same kind of capable agent that software
developers now have. That agent needs to work with books, notes, research, drawings, experiments,
and memory instead of treating every question as an isolated chat.

So Buddy can read PDFs and EPUBs, open an existing Obsidian vault, research questions, draw on a
whiteboard, build simulations and games, and create quizzes or flashcards from what you are learning.

That is deliberately a lot. Real use will show which parts become essential and which parts should
fade away. I would rather learn that from people bringing their own material into Buddy than decide
it from a feature checklist.

The first launch is for curious desktop users who already move between books, notes, whiteboards,
and AI tools. Buddy is local-first, keeps its work in files on your computer, and lets you connect
ChatGPT, an API key, or a local model.

It is free, requires no account, and is available today for macOS and Windows.

Download Buddy at hibuddy.in and try it with something you care about learning.

Then tell me two things: what became useful, and what got in your way. That feedback will shape what
Buddy becomes next.

## Guardrails

- Write in first person.
- Use one concrete founder memory or question.
- Keep the launch announcement near the top.
- Use two or three product proofs, not the entire feature inventory.
- Do not attack Obsidian, Kindle, Notion, or other tools.
- Call Buddy an AI agent, not a learning superapp.
- Do not say Buddy is open source or that everything stays local.
- Use one final action: download or feedback.
- Do not publish a model or provider count until `50+` versus `150+` is reconciled.
